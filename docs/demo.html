Drag the rule to cut the document anywhere. At each cut the reader says whether it is waiting for more, and what it would say if the text ended there — the two answers a parser has to give when its text arrives in pieces. Delete a bracket and watch the expectation change.

bench b — draw the table's tree —

The tree is what make_expr_parser built from the table: precedence and associativity are visible in its shape, not asserted afterwards. A boxed operator is a node, the lines under it are its operands, and the left-to-right order is the order the text was read in.

The glue is twelve lines of MoonBit. Each bench calls one function: cut(text, at) and expression(text), both taking a string and returning a string. Everything the page draws is computed by the library at the moment you type — the refusals, the positions, the expectations, and the tree.
